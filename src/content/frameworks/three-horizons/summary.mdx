## What it is

Three Horizons asks one question about money and attention: are we starving the core to fund
the future, or starving the future to protect the core?

Horizon one is the business as it earns today. Horizon two is what is already working and
needs scaling. Horizon three is the set of options that are not businesses yet and may never
be. Every initiative belongs to exactly one of them, and the framework's output is an
allocation — of budget, of people, of the founder's week — across the three.

The allocation is the finding. Companies rarely have a strategy problem at this level; they
have an arithmetic problem they have never written down. Three horizons of ambition and one
horizon of funding is the most common shape in the world, and it is invisible until the
numbers sit side by side on the same page.

## When it earns its place

Run it when there is a portfolio to balance — several initiatives, one pool of resource, and
an argument about sequencing that keeps restarting. Run it after the direction frameworks
have produced candidate options, because a horizon map of ideas nobody has costed is a mood
board.

Do not run it annually as a ritual. The horizons move at the speed of the business, not the
speed of the calendar, and re-running it on a schedule produces the same three columns with
last year's initiatives shuffled between them.

> A third horizon funded out of whatever is left over at the end of the quarter is not a
> horizon. It is a hobby with a slide.

The other failure is treating the horizons as maturity stages that everything must climb
through. They are not a pipeline. Some horizon three options are meant to be killed, and
killing them cheaply is the value they were bought for.

What good looks like: allocation stated as a share of something finite, an explicit note on
what each horizon is being starved of, and time-phasing that says when the balance is
supposed to change. It writes a horizon portfolio into the shared state, which the scorecard
and the quarter's objectives both read — so the argument about sequencing only has to happen
once.
