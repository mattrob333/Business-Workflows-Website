## What it is

Kano sorts features by the shape of the satisfaction they produce, which turns out to matter
more than how much satisfaction they produce.

A must-be feature earns nothing when present and costs everything when absent — nobody
praises a login that works. A performance feature pays out roughly in proportion to how well
it is done, which makes it the only category where "improve it" is straightforward advice. A
delighter earns disproportionately when present and nothing at all when missing, because
customers do not know to ask for it. Two further categories keep the model honest:
indifferent, where effort produces no response at all, and reverse, where some segment
actively dislikes what another segment wanted.

The practical consequence is that a single ranked backlog is the wrong instrument. Polishing
a must-be past working is spending money to buy nothing. Ignoring one is losing customers
without any signal that you are.

## When it earns its place

Run it when a roadmap is contested and every item on it is defensible. Run it after the fit
work, on features that already exist or are already specified — Kano classifies, it does not
invent.

Segment it. The reverse category exists because the same feature genuinely lands differently
for different buyers, and an aggregate classification hides exactly the disagreement you ran
the framework to find.

> A classification drawn from conversation is a hypothesis, and it stays one until a survey
> disagrees with it or fails to.

That is the rule this site enforces rather than merely recommends. Interview transcripts are
excellent at suggesting which category a feature is in and unreliable at confirming it, so a
Kano output from transcripts alone is labelled hypothesis and carries the label into every
framework downstream. The structured survey — functional and dysfunctional question pairs —
is what promotes it.

What good looks like: classifications per segment, each with the evidence type behind it,
delighters treated as perishable because today's delighter is next year's must-be, and an
explicit indifferent list. That last one is the cheapest output in strategy: a written record
of the work that nobody would have noticed.
