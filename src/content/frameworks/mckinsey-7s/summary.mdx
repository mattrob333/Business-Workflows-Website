## What it is

McKinsey 7S asks whether the organisation, as it is actually built, can carry the strategy
that was just chosen.

Seven elements: strategy, structure and systems — the hard ones, the ones that appear in
documents — and shared values, skills, style and staff, the soft ones, which decide what
actually happens on a Tuesday. The framework's claim is that the seven are wired to each
other, so a change to any one of them puts tension on the rest, and a strategy that ignores
that wiring is a strategy the organisation will quietly decline.

The output is a comparison, not a score: where each element is now, where the chosen strategy
requires it to be, and what stands between those two states. The gaps are the useful part.
They are usually not capability gaps at all — they are incentive gaps, reporting-line gaps,
or a shared value that contradicts the plan in a way everyone can feel and nobody has said.

## When it earns its place

Run it after a direction is chosen and before anyone writes objectives against it. Run it
when a good strategy has failed twice for reasons nobody can name; that pattern is almost
always an unexamined element pulling against the other six.

Do not run it as an org-health survey. Seven elements scored out of ten produces a heptagon
that looks like insight and answers no question, because the framework's value is entirely
in the *tension between* elements, not in the level of any one of them.

> Structure and staff will beat strategy every time, and they will do it politely, in
> meetings, over about nine months.

The second failure is scoring the soft four from the top of the building. Style and shared
values are observed, not declared; the evidence is where escalations actually go, what gets
celebrated, and which rule everyone knows is safe to break.

What good looks like: current and required states written for each element against a named
strategy, the two or three real tensions singled out from the merely imperfect, and each gap
attached to something someone can change — a reporting line, a compensation rule, an
approval threshold. The output is an organisational alignment read plus a refreshed set of
capability gaps, which the delegation and measurement frameworks both read.
