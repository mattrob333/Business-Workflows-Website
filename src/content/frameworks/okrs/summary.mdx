## What it is

An objective says what the quarter is for. Key results say how you will know it happened.
That is the entire mechanism, and almost every failure of OKRs is a failure to keep those two
sentences apart.

An objective is qualitative and singular: *installation throughput stops being the thing that
limits the business.* A key result is a measurable end state with a baseline, a target, an
owner and a date — not an activity. "Run a scheduling pilot" is work. "Median wait from
signature to crew start falls below the level it was at two years ago" is a result, and the
difference is that only one of them can fail while everybody was busy.

The output is a tree: strategy, scorecard objective, team objective, key results, and the
initiatives underneath them, with confidence tracked and dependencies named.

## When it earns its place

Run it after the constraint is known. This is the ordering the whole site argues for: a
quarter's objectives written against a diagnosis are a plan, and the same objectives written
against everything at once are a wish list distributed evenly across the org chart.

Run it quarterly, and re-run it when the constraint moves rather than when the calendar says
so.

> A key result you can hit by working harder was a task. A key result you can only hit by
> changing something was an objective all along.

The classic failure is volume. Five objectives with five key results each is twenty-five
promises, which is the same as none, and it happens because every team wants its work
represented. Representation is not the purpose. Two objectives that would genuinely change
the shape of the business are worth more than a document in which everyone can find
themselves.

The second failure is grading them like a performance review, which reliably teaches people
to set targets they can already reach.

What good looks like: few objectives, key results with baselines as well as targets, every
one owned by a named person, confidence updated honestly through the quarter, and at least
one that is genuinely at risk. The output is objectives and work assignments — which is what
the delegation framework picks up when it decides who, or what, does each piece of it.
