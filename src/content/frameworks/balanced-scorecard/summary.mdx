## What it is

The Balanced Scorecard exists because financial measures are lagging measures. By the time
revenue tells you something is wrong, the thing that was wrong happened two quarters ago and
has already been forgotten.

So it asks for measures across four perspectives — financial, customer, internal process, and
learning and growth — and, crucially, for the causal chain between them. Better technician
training raises first-visit completion; first-visit completion raises the customer's renewal
rate; renewal raises revenue. Written down, that chain is a testable claim about how this
business converts effort into money. Left unwritten, the four perspectives are just four
lists, and the framework has been reduced to a dashboard with a philosophy attached.

Leading and lagging is the distinction that carries the whole model. Every perspective should
contain at least one measure that moves *before* the outcome it predicts.

## When it earns its place

Run it once a strategy and an organisational read exist, so the measures attach to something
specific. Run it before the quarter's objectives are written, because objectives that are
not anchored to a scorecard measure drift toward whatever is easiest to count.

Run it only if you are willing to wire it to a system that already produces the data.

> A scorecard nobody writes to does not go wrong. It goes quiet, keeps its last value, and
> is believed for another year.

That is this framework's specific, famous decay. The measures are chosen in a good meeting,
entered by hand for two quarters, then entered late, then entered from memory, then not at
all — and the report keeps rendering. The check is mechanical: for every measure, name the
system it comes from and the frequency it lands. Any measure whose answer is "someone
compiles it" has a half-life.

What good looks like: few measures rather than many, every one with a source system and a
cadence, an explicit cause-and-effect map linking the perspectives, and at least one measure
that would embarrass someone if it moved the wrong way. The output — scorecard objectives and
leading indicators — is what makes the constraint framework re-runnable rather than an annual
event.
